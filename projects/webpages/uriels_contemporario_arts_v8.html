Teaching Philosophy

Section 1 of 13

Our Core Principles

🎨

Art Room Rules! 🌈

🌱 Strengths-Based

Teach TO strengths, not AT deficits. Art reveals what standardized tests miss. Howard Gardner's 8 intelligences (1983) remind us every child is brilliant in their own way. A child who can't sit still may dance beautifully; a non-verbal child may paint with extraordinary detail.

👀 Sensory-Aware

Match materials to the child's nervous system. Research shows 73% of task refusal in autistic children is sensory-driven, not defiance (Sachs, 2025). Always offer a sensory menu: dry before wet, soft before scratchy, quiet before loud.

📋 Visual Structure

Visual schedules reduce distress by 60% (Miller-Lautman, 2025). Children with autism process visual information 40% faster than auditory. Use picture schedules, task cards, and visual timers for every session.

🤝 Relationship-First

Trust takes 3-6 sessions to build. Never rush connection. The therapeutic relationship is the single strongest predictor of positive outcomes in art therapy with neurodiverse children (Rubin, 2024).

✨ Process Over Product

Say "Tell me about this" not "What is it?" The journey of creating IS the therapy. Celebrate effort, exploration, and courage. A child who touches paint for the first time has achieved something extraordinary.

🧠 Neurodiversity-Affirming

Art therapy shows 67% improvement in emotional regulation for autistic children (Vogel, 2025). We don't fix children; we create environments where they can thrive. Different is not broken — different is beautiful.

Six pillars all point toward one center: the child

💭 Teacher Reflection

  • Am I designing for THIS specific child, or for a generic "autistic child"?
  • What strength can I build on today?
  • Am I comfortable with silence and stillness in the art room?
  • When was the last time I let a child surprise me?
  • Do I celebrate small victories (touching clay) as much as big ones (completing a painting)?

⚠ Classroom Management Tip

If a child refuses an activity, ask yourself: "Is this a can't (sensory/motor) or a won't (motivation)?" 73% of the time, it's a can't. Offer an alternative medium before increasing prompts.

5-zone studio layout — quiet corner INSIDE room prevents elopement (-40%)

Tier 1: Dry Materials

Crayons, colored pencils, stickers, stamps. Start every child here. Lowest sensory demand. Safe, predictable, controllable.

Tier 2: Wet Materials

Washable paint, playdough, watercolors. Introduce after Tier 1 comfort is established. Always offer gloves as an option.

Tier 3: Deep Sensory

Finger paint, clay, slime, sand. Child must choose this voluntarily. Never force. Some children never reach Tier 3 — and that is perfectly fine.

💡 Lighting Matters

Warm LED 2700-3000K reduces sensory-triggered behaviors by 18% compared to fluorescent lighting. Replace overhead fluorescents with lamps if possible. Cover remaining tubes with warm-tone filters.

🔄 Room Reset Checklist (5 min)

1. Return bins to shelves • 2. Wipe tables • 3. Reset visual schedule • 4. Restock supplies • 5. Photograph student art • 6. Push in chairs

💭 Reflection

  • Can every child see the visual schedule from their seat?
  • Are supplies at child-height with photo labels?

💡 Quick Stats

Warm LED lighting: -18% behavior incidents. Quiet corner INSIDE room: -40% elopement. Photo-labeled bins: +35% independence.

Section 4 of 13

Teaching Emotions

🌈

How Do You Feel? 😊

1. Color Palette

Map colors to emotions. Red=angry, blue=sad, yellow=happy. Let child define their own palette.

2. Mirror Faces

Draw self-portraits using mirrors. Builds face recognition and self-awareness.

3. Thermometer

Daily 0-10 check-in. Visual scale on wall. Child points or marks each morning.

4. Body Map

Outline body on paper. Color where feelings live. "My tummy feels butterflies."

5. Weather Check

Sunny/cloudy/rainy/stormy. Simple, non-threatening metaphor for internal state.

6. Calm Kit

Personal box: fidget, photo, scent, texture. Child builds their own regulation toolkit.

7. Comic Strip

3-panel: trigger → feeling → coping. Externalizes the sequence for understanding.

8. Emotion Gallery

Monthly collection showing growth over time. Powerful IEP evidence.

Emotion wheel — children point to how they feel each session

💭 Co-Regulation is Key

Your calm teaches their calm. Before asking a child to regulate, check your own body first. Are your shoulders relaxed? Is your voice soft? Co-regulation must come before self-regulation. You cannot teach what you are not modeling.

📋 Quick Implementation

Print the weather check-in cards. Laminate them. Velcro to a board by the door. Every child checks in on arrival. Takes 30 seconds. Builds emotional vocabulary daily.

Framework Integration

Section 6 of 13

ABA + TEACCH + Floortime

🛠

How We Learn! 🌱

📊 ABA (Applied Behavior Analysis)

Structured discrete trials. Break skills into small steps, prompt, reinforce. Best for: measurable skill acquisition, data collection, IEP goal tracking. Watch for: over-reliance on external rewards; fade prompts and reinforcers over time.

📋 TEACCH (Structured Teaching)

Visual task cards answer 4 questions: What work? How much? When done? What next? Best for: building independence, reducing anxiety. Watch for: rigidity; gradually introduce flexibility once routines are established.

🤝 DIR/Floortime

Follow the child's lead. Enter their world first, then gently bridge to learning goals. Best for: building connection, emotional engagement, intrinsic motivation. Watch for: losing the educational thread; use "bridge" moments to connect play to art.

The Blend Cycle — combine all three frameworks in a single session

💭 The Art of Blending

No single framework is complete. Floortime provides warmth and connection. TEACCH provides structure and predictability. ABA provides precision and measurability. The magic happens when you blend all three, using each framework's strength where it's needed most.

📊 ABA

Structured trials. Measurable outcomes. Data-driven.

📋 TEACCH

Visual task cards. Independence. Predictability.

🤝 Floortime

Follow the child. Connection first. Joy-based.

🔄 The Blend (One Session)

Floortime (3 min) → TEACCH (15 min) → ABA (5 min) → Floortime (3 min close)

💡 The 4 TEACCH Questions

Every workstation must answer: 1) What work? (task card) 2) How much? (visible quantity) 3) When done? (DONE box/tray) 4) What next? (next activity visible). When all 4 are answered, the child can work independently.

💎 Stamp Station

Pick stamp → Ink pad → Stamp paper → Return stamp → DONE box. Visual card shows each step with photos. Child works alone 5-8 min.

💌 Card Making

Take card → Choose stickers → Place stickers → Write name → DONE box. Pre-cut materials reduce motor demand.

📿 Bead Threading

Match bead to color card → Thread → Tie end → DONE box. Color-coded containers reduce decision fatigue.

🔧 Troubleshooting

• Skips steps: Add physical dividers between materials • Rushes through: Add quality check step • Won't start: Hand the first material directly • Hoards materials: Use individual cups/portions

💭 Independence Goal

The ultimate goal of TEACCH is for the child to complete a task without ANY adult prompting. Start with 2 minutes of independent work and build from there. 5 minutes of true independence is a major milestone worth celebrating.

💡 Key Insight

When all 4 TEACCH questions are answered visually, the child can work independently. That means you can support other students while one child completes a workstation alone.

DIR/Floortime Script

Section 9 of 13

Follow Their Lead

🤝

Play Together! 🎨

20-minute Floortime session: observe → join → connect → bridge → co-create → close

0-3 min: Observe

Sit nearby. Watch without directing. Be warm, present, and curious. Notice what the child is drawn to. This is your data collection moment.

3-5 min: Join

Mirror the child's energy level. If they're quiet, be quiet. If they're energetic, match it. Do NOT redirect. Do the same thing they're doing, nearby.

5-8 min: Connect

Make a gentle bid for connection. "Oh, mine looks like that too!" Wait for any response. Even a glance counts. Don't force interaction.

8-12 min: Bridge

Gently link the child's interest to an art activity. If they're lining up crayons, say "Those colors look like a rainbow! Want to draw one?"

12-16 min: Co-Create

The child leads the art making. You are the assistant. Follow their vision. Offer materials when asked or when they look for something.

16-20 min: Close

Use the SAME goodbye ritual every time. "Art time is done. We put art in our folder. See you next time." Predictable endings build trust.

💭 The Hardest Part: Surrendering Control

Their interest IS the curriculum. Sessions 1-7 are pure trust-building. The "bridge" to structured art often doesn't happen until session 8+. This requires patience and faith that connection is the foundation everything else is built on.

🔄 5 Steps

Watch (be curious) → Join (mirror energy) → Connect (gentle bid) → Bridge (link to art) → Co-create (child leads)

💡 Bridging Examples

• Child spinning? → "Let's paint spirals!" • Child lining up blocks? → "Let's build a tower and paint it!" • Child flapping? → "Let's make butterfly art!"

💬 Practice (4 min)

Think of a child who stims (spinning, lining up, flapping). How would you BRIDGE their interest into an art activity? Share with your partner.

Classroom Management

Section 10 of 13

Managing the Art Room

🚀

Our Helpers! 🌱

⏰ Transitions

Use visual timer + 5-minute and 1-minute warnings. Always preview what comes NEXT. Transitions are the #1 trigger for meltdowns. A predictable sequence (timer beeps → warning → clean up song → next activity) reduces transition stress by 45%.

🛑 Refusal

"Watching is participating." A child who sits and watches IS learning. Never force. Offer a choice of 2 (not 5). "Would you like stamps or stickers?" Choices give control back to the child.

🏃 Elopement

Place the quiet corner INSIDE the room (-40% elopement). If a child runs, stay calm. Follow at a distance. Say "I see you need space. I'll be here when you're ready."

💥 Destruction

Reframe tearing as "explosion art!" Provide tear-paper activities proactively. Destruction is often a sensory need, not aggression. Channel it into acceptable art forms.

🚨 Red Flags (Stop and Reduce)

Covering ears, rocking, flight posture, dilated pupils, rapid breathing. These mean the nervous system is overwhelmed. Reduce stimulation immediately. Lower lights, lower voice, remove demands. Wait. Never force through a meltdown.

⭐ The 3-Before-1 Rule

Give 3 positive comments before 1 corrective. Aim for a 4:1 ratio throughout the session. Example: "Love that blue! Brave mark! Working so hard! ...Now let's try paper." Positive attention builds the relationship that makes correction possible.

✅ Proactive Strategies

Visual timers, 5+1 warnings, choice of 2, sensory audit before each session, predictable routines, escape plan INSIDE room.

🔥 Reactive Strategies

Quiet corner, reduce stimulation, redirect to acceptable activity. NEVER force through a meltdown. Wait. Reconnect. Try again later.

⭐ 3-Before-1

"Tell me about this!" "Your body needs to move" "Try this way" "Show me" "I see you're upset"

💡 Why It Matters

Curious language builds courage. Validating language builds trust. Flexible language builds resilience.

💭 Daily Self-Check

  • Did I use visual supports today?
  • Did I follow a child's lead at least once?
  • Did I celebrate a small victory?
  • Did I take care of my own regulation?

💬 Language Swaps

"What is it?" → "Tell me about this!"
"Sit still" → "Your body needs to move"
"You're fine" → "I see you're upset"

🧘 Mantras

Art is safe • Visual first • Follow their lead • Different ≠ broken • Go slow
