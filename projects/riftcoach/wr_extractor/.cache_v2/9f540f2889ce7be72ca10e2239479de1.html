Wild Rift content
Enchanter
Controllers are defensive casters that oversee the battlefield by protecting and opening up opportunities for their allies.

Enchanters focus on amplifying their allies' effectiveness by directly augmenting them and defending them from incoming threats. Enchanters themselves are often quite fragile and bring relatively low damage to the table, meaning they really only shine when grouped together with others.

Examples
  • Janna
  • Sona
  • Soraka

Wild Rift content
Support
Within a drafted team, the Support position is one of two champions pushing into the Dragon lane. Securing the Dragon lane will allow access to the Dragon, an objective that can influence the rest of the game. However, the Support may not be able to grow at the same rate of other positions due to a lack of income, and will instead prefer efficiency to their power.
A Support champion will work best by either supplying utility or setting up opportunities to kill the enemy.

Suitable champions include Catchers, Enchanters, Vanguards Wardens.

Wild Rift content
Mana

 Mana (also known as MP) is a stat that is the resource of many champions necessary to cast most of their abilities. Mana can be restored via mana regeneration and some abilities, items, runes, and buffs.

Mana is represented in-game by the blue bar within the HUD, indicating the champion's current mana and maximum mana. Current mana is reduced by expenditure through paying an ability's mana cost.

Wild Rift content
Ranged

Ranged champions have a basic attack  range usually beyond 300 units. Many deal damage through projectiles, which are subject to mitigating effects like Yasuo's Wind Wall. Some champions can shift between a ranged and melee form, and certain items/runes differ for ranged characters.

Wild Rift content
Attack speed ratio

The Attack speed ratio (AS ratio) adjusts the effectiveness of bonus AS from all sources. It is usually equal to the unit's base AS (level 1 with no bonuses), but tuning to a higher or lower relative value translates to more or less bonus AS received compared to other units with the same base AS.

Total AS = Base AS + Bonus AS × AS Ratio
(All values in decimal form)

The ingame stats HUD displays the AS ratio instead of base AS to communicate this comparative effectiveness. The Wiki opts to always list both stats and show the AS ratio as "N/A" if the two are equal.

Wild Rift content
Edge range

Edge range is calculated from the edge of a source unit's hitbox to the edge of a target unit's hitbox.

  • Edge range is mostly used for basic attacks; skill shots; and point-blank areas of effect (including auras).
  • These become more effective against large champions, as the source gains more reach against them. This may be favorable or unfavorable: durable champions tend to be larger and thus easier to hit, and frail champions tend to be smaller and more difficult to hit.
Wild Rift content
Untargetable
Effects cannot hit nor target a unit that is untargetable. Effects that have already hit will not be invalidated, unless they require targetability.

Becoming untargetable will cause targeted inputs to be interrupted and non-champions to lose aggro. Most untargetability will also destroy incoming targeted projectiles.

Wild Rift content
On-attack
On-attack effects refer to various instances that are triggered by the user when they finish their basic attack windup.

Unlike on-hit effects, on-attack effects do not require the user to land their basic attacks, only to finish their windup. Abilities that trigger on-attack effects will do so upon successfully hitting the target.

League of Legends content
Default damage
Default damage is dealt by effects that are not otherwise classified, or are deliberately left unclassified. This most common source of default damage is splash damage from basic attacks.

Default damage does not interact with most other effects, such as Conqueror stacking, Luden's Echo or Arcane Comet. The main exception is:

  • Omnivamp and Physical vamp (where applicable) heal for a percentage of damage dealt.

Effects triggered by damage subtypes ( physical, magic, true) will always occur regardless of damage type (e.g. Black Cleaver's Carve stacking on instances of physical damage and Morellonomicon's Grievous Wounds application on instances of magic damage).

Wild Rift content
On-hit effect
On-hit effect is an umbrella term for many effects that trigger when you deal an instance of basic damage, such as a basic attack.

Most on-hit effects deal proc damage or default damage (aka splash damage), which are non-reactive (i.e. the effects themselves don't trigger other effects).

Wild Rift content
Enchantment redirection

A certain group of damaging abilities called enchantments or empowerments require interaction of an empowerer, with an ally hitting an enemy. Enchantments commonly include mark detonations or buffs given to allies. The credit for an applied enchantment is directed in the following ways:

  • Any damage dealt through the ally by the enchantment is credited to the empowerer instead of the ally. Effects that can trigger on-damage instances of the empowerer (e.g. Aery, Morellonomicon's Grievous Wounds on magic damage, etc.) will trigger and also be credited to the empowerer. For the empowerer's redirected damage only, the ally will apply the empowerer's damage modifiers (e.g.  magic penetration).
  • Any unit kill achieved by the ally having applied the empowerer's redirected damage, is credited to the ally instead of the empowerer.
Wild Rift content
Turret

A turret, also called a tower, is a fortification that defends a part of territory. It provides vision of the area and autonomously fires homing bolts at nearby enemies, establishing a defensive ground for allies.

Turrets are most notable in Classic, defending against waves of enemy Minions from their ultimate goal of destroying the nexus. Champions work to destroy enemy turrets to push their assault into the enemies' territory. Turrets visually fall apart as they take damage.

Wild Rift content
Ward

Wards are deployable units that that grant sight over the surrounding area. They can only be destroyed by basic attacks, granting gold and  experience.
There are three kinds of deployable wards available for purchase: Control Ward and Warding Totem.

Wild Rift content
Slow
The target has reduced  move speed.

Slows stack at a diminished rate. The highest amount from one source will apply in full, during which all subsequent slows will reduce to 30% effectiveness. Slow duration will refresh from the same effect.

League of Legends content
Area damage

Area damage is the damage type dealt by most abilities and some items and runes that can hit targets within a zone. Area damage applies spell effects by default.

  • Jax's Counter Strike grants him 25% damage reduction against all incoming area damage.
  • Many champion summoned units such as Elise's Spiderlings, Naafiri's Packmates, and Yorick's Mist Walkers take reduced damage from area damage effects.
  •  Omnivamp heals with 33.3% effectiveness on instances of area damage against non-champions.

Effects triggered by damage subtypes ( physical, magic, true) will always occur regardless of damage type (e.g. Black Cleaver's Carve stacking on instances of physical damage and Morellonomicon's Grievous Wounds application on instances of magic damage).

Wild Rift content
Polymorph

The target is silenced and turned into a helpless critter.

Silence: The target loses control of their abilities and items.

Polymorph is the signature effect of Lulu's Whimsy, which also disarms the target and reduces their move speed.

Interrupts: Interrupts attack windups (including uncancellable) and channel abilities.
Wild Rift content
True sight

True Sight reveals units through Fog of War, brush, and stealth. It does not bypass nearsight effects.

Wild Rift content
Disarm

The target loses control of their attacks.

Interrupts: Interrupts basic attack windups.
Wild Rift content
Slow resist

Slow resist is a stat that directly reduces the effectiveness of slows.

Wild Rift content
Shield
A shield adds hitpoints over the unit's  health that absorbs the next instances of damage in place of actual health. This can be improved by various items and effects depending on the type of shielding.

Incoming shields can trigger bonus effects from Shield Bash. Outgoing shields can trigger bonus effects from Aery, Ardent Censer, and Staff of Flowing Waters. Shield reduction effects, exclusively Serpent's Fang, reduce all incoming and outgoing shields.

League of Legends content
Spell damage

Spell damage is the damage type dealt by most abilities and some items and runes that affect targets directly, usually a single one. Spell damage applies spell effects by default.

Effects triggered by damage subtypes ( physical, magic, true) will always occur regardless of damage type (e.g. Black Cleaver's Carve stacking on instances of physical damage and Morellonomicon's Grievous Wounds application on instances of magic damage).

Wild Rift content
Spell shield
Spell shield can negate incoming hostile abilities from applying to the recipient. This includes many active effects coming from champion abilities, epic monsters, champion summoned units, and items, but almost none from runes and summoner spells.

The shielding can vary per case with other included mechanisms such as on-hit effects and subsequent targeting
(e.g. Bouncing, Tethers).

Wild Rift content
Airborne

The target is forcefully displaced, either upward (knockup) or across a distance (knockback), during which they lose control of their movement, attacks, abilities, and items.

Interrupts: Interrupts attack windups and channel abilities. The forced displacement interrupts dashes and overrides previous airborne effects.
