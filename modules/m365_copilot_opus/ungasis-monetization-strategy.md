## 1. When NOT to Monetize Yet

### 🍳 The Kitchen Analogy: Do Not Charge for Food You Have Not Tasted

Imagine you learned a new recipe yesterday. You cooked it once and it turned out... okay. Would you:

❌ Open a restaurant tomorrow and charge customers?
❌ Print a menu with prices before anyone has tasted the food?
❌ Set up a cash register before you even know if people like the dish?

**Of course not.** You would let friends taste it first. Get feedback. Improve the recipe. Then — once people say *"I would pay for this"* — THEN you charge.

### 🛑 Do NOT Monetize When:

| # | Situation | Why Not | What to Do Instead |
|---|-----------|---------|-------------------|
| 1 | **You have not talked to real users yet** | You do not know if the problem is real or painful enough | Go back to validation — survey 10 people |
| 2 | **Fewer than 5 people have tested your app** | Not enough evidence that this works | Invite more testers, collect feedback |
| 3 | **Nobody has said "I would pay for this"** | Willingness to pay is unproven | Ask directly, or test with a waitlist |
| 4 | **Your app still has major bugs** | Charging for a broken product damages trust | Fix the core flow first |
| 5 | **You have not defined a refund policy** | If someone pays and wants money back, you have no plan | Write a simple refund policy before accepting money |
| 6 | **You have not thought about taxes** | Earning money creates tax obligations | Get general awareness (see Section 12) |
| 7 | **You are adding payments just because you can** | Payment complexity adds bugs, support burden, and legal risk | Only add payments when value is proven |
| 8 | **The app is still a personal tool nobody else uses** | Portfolio project ≠ monetizable product | Keep it as portfolio; monetize when others want it |
| 9 | **You do not have a support channel** | Users who pay expect help when things break | Set up email, chat, or form before charging |
| 10 | **You have not reviewed privacy, security, or data handling** | Handling money means higher responsibility | Complete the security checklist first |

### The Golden Rule

> **Do not add payments until at least 3 real people say: "I would pay for this." And mean it.**

## 2. How to Validate Willingness to Pay — WITHOUT Building Payments

You do NOT need to build a payment system to test if people will pay. Use these methods first.

| # | Method | How It Works | Cost | Time | What You Learn |
|---|--------|-------------|------|------|---------------|
| 1 | **"Would you pay?" question** | In user interviews, simply ask: "If this existed and cost ₱X/month, would you pay?" | Free | 5 min per person | Direct signal — but people often say yes and then do not pay |
| 2 | **Pricing page test** | Add a pricing page to your prototype with fake prices. Track how many people click "Sign Up" or "Get Started" | Free | 1–2 hours to build | Click-through rate shows real interest |
| 3 | **Waitlist with tier selection** | Create a form: "Join the waitlist. Which plan interests you? Free / ₱299/mo / ₱599/mo" | Free (Tally) | 30 min | See which tier people choose |
| 4 | **Pre-order or deposit** | "Pay ₱100 deposit to reserve early access. Full refund if you do not like it." | Free to set up | 1 hour | Strongest validation — they opened their wallet |
| 5 | **Concierge MVP** | Manually deliver the service (you do the work, not the app). See if they pay YOU for it. | Free | 1–2 hours per customer | Validates the VALUE, not just the TOOL |
| 6 | **Competitive pricing research** | Find 3–5 similar tools. What do they charge? | Free | 1 hour | Shows what the market pays for this type of solution |

### 🍳 Analogy

> **A pricing page is like putting a price tag on the menu in your food stall window.** You are not cooking yet — you are seeing if people stop and look. If they walk past, change the menu. If they walk in, you know you have something.

## 3. $0-Upfront Revenue Tests — 10 Methods

These are ways to test revenue WITHOUT paying for tools upfront. All use free or transaction-fee-only tools.

| # | Method | Description | Difficulty | Best For | Example | Risk |
|---|--------|-------------|-----------|---------|---------|------|
| 1 | **Waitlist / Interest Form** | Collect emails of people who want early access. Measures demand before building. | ⭐ Easy | Any idea — first validation step | Tally form: "Join waitlist for [App Name]" | Low — no money involved yet |
| 2 | **Manual Demo / Concierge MVP** | You personally deliver the service to 1–5 customers. No app needed. | ⭐ Easy | Service-based ideas, consulting | "I will organize your lesson notes for you this week — ₱500" | Low — but time-intensive |
| 3 | **Consulting / Service-First Offer** | Sell your expertise as a service before building a product. | ⭐⭐ Medium | Skills-based solopreneurs | "I will audit your workflow and recommend tools — ₱2,000" | Low — you are selling time |
| 4 | **Paid Pilot** | Offer the MVP to 3–5 paying users at a discount in exchange for feedback. | ⭐⭐ Medium | Apps with clear value proposition | "₱199/mo for 3 months — includes direct support + input on features" | Medium — you commit to delivering |
| 5 | **Template / Digital Product** | Sell a Notion template, spreadsheet, checklist, or guide — not an app. | ⭐ Easy | Content creators, knowledge workers | Notion template: "Client Management System — ₱299 one-time" | Low — one-time sale, no recurring support |
| 6 | **Newsletter / Community Lead Magnet** | Build audience with free content → upsell premium content or tools | ⭐⭐ Medium | Long-term audience building | Free weekly email → paid deep-dive newsletter | Low — but slow returns |
| 7 | **Donation / Tip Model** | Offer the tool for free, add a "Buy Me a Coffee" or tip link | ⭐ Easy | Open source, community tools | "If this tool helped you, buy me a coffee ☕" | Very low — but income is unpredictable |
| 8 | **Marketplace** | Sell on an existing marketplace (Gumroad, LemonSqueezy, Etsy Digital) | ⭐⭐ Medium | Digital products, templates, guides | List a Figma template on Gumroad | Low — marketplace handles payments |
| 9 | **Affiliate / Referral** | Recommend tools you use. Earn commission when someone signs up through your link. | ⭐ Easy | Content creators, reviewers | "I use Supabase for my projects — sign up with my link" | Very low — but small income |
| 10 | **Simple SaaS Subscription** | Monthly subscription for an app. Only when validated and justified. | ⭐⭐⭐ Hard | Apps with proven repeated use + willingness to pay | "₱299/mo — unlimited student profiles, session notes, reminders" | Higher — ongoing support, billing, refunds |

### Progression Path

```
Waitlist → Manual Demo → Paid Pilot → Digital Product or SaaS Subscription
```

> **Start at the LEFT. Move RIGHT only when you have evidence at each step.**

## 5. Philippines-Specific Monetization Notes

> **⚠️ This is general planning guidance for Manila/PH context. It is NOT tax, legal, or accounting advice. Get qualified local review before real commercial launch.**

### 🇵🇭 Key PH Considerations

| Topic | Details | Action |
|-------|---------|--------|
| **Stripe availability** | Stripe global availability page did NOT list the Philippines as a supported country in the checked official source (May 2026) | Do not default to Stripe. Use PayMongo for PH local, Lemon Squeezy for global. |
| **PayMongo** | Official pricing verified: no setup/monthly/hidden fees. PH payment methods: cards, GCash, Maya, GrabPay, bank transfer. Rates vary by method. | Good PH-local option. Check business registration requirements. |
| **Lemon Squeezy** | Official pricing: $0/month, 5% + $0.50 per transaction. Merchant of Record handles global taxes. | Strong for global digital product sales. |
| **Lemon Squeezy international payout fee** | ⚠️ The Master Prompt claimed 1% international payout fee for Philippines. **This was NOT verified from an official Lemon Squeezy source.** | ⚠️ Manually confirm before financial planning. Budget for ~6–6.5% effective fee until verified. |
| **Lemon Squeezy payout details** | ⚠️ Exact payout schedule, minimum payout, and supported PH bank details were not fully verified in this pass. | ⚠️ Check Lemon Squeezy payout settings manually before relying on specific numbers. |
| **Tax obligations** | Earning money in the Philippines creates tax obligations (income tax, percentage tax, or VAT depending on revenue). BIR registration may be required. | Consult a PH accountant. Do NOT ignore this. |
| **Business registration** | Depending on revenue and business structure, DTI/SEC/BIR registration may be needed. | Research requirements before scaling beyond paid pilots. |
| **Peso pricing** | If selling to Filipino customers, pricing in PHP is better for conversion. Lemon Squeezy supports multi-currency. | Use PHP for PH market, USD for global market. |
| **GCash/Maya** | Very popular payment methods in PH. PayMongo supports both. Lemon Squeezy does NOT (as of this writing — verify). | If your customers are PH-based, PayMongo may have better conversion rates. |
| **Data Privacy Act** | Philippines has the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (RA 10173). Collecting personal data from PH users requires compliance. | Add a privacy notice. Know what data you collect and why. |

### 💰 PH Payment Method Rates (via PayMongo — verify on paymongo.com/pricing)

| Payment Method | Rate | Notes |
|---------------|------|-------|
| Cards (Visa, Mastercard) | ~3.5% + ₱15 | Standard card processing |
| GCash | ~2.5% + ₱15 | Very common in PH |
| Maya | ~2.5% + ₱15 | Growing payment method |
| GrabPay | ~2.5% + ₱15 | For Grab users |

## 6. Commercial Readiness Gate — 10 Criteria

> **Do NOT move from "revenue test" to "commercial product/SaaS" unless ALL 10 of these are true.**

| # | Criterion | What It Means (Simple) | How to Check |
|---|-----------|----------------------|-------------|
| 1 | **At least 5–10 target users validated the problem** | Real people (not your friends being nice) confirmed the problem exists and is painful | Survey results, interview notes, feedback forms |
| 2 | **At least 3 users showed serious willingness to pay** | They said "yes I would pay" AND took an action (clicked pricing, joined waitlist tier, made deposit) | Waitlist tier selections, pricing page clicks, deposits |
| 3 | **The tool solves a repeated painful problem** | The problem happens regularly (daily/weekly), not just once | User interviews confirm frequency |
| 4 | **You can explain the value in one sentence** | Your Product North Star is clear and compelling | Write it. Read it to someone. Do they get it instantly? |
| 5 | **Support burden is manageable** | You can handle questions, bugs, and complaints from paying users | Estimate: how many support messages per week? Can you handle them? |
| 6 | **Security and privacy risks are understood** | You know what data you collect, where it is stored, and how it is protected | Completed SECURITY_CHECKLIST.md; RLS enabled; secrets safe |
| 7 | **Payment, refund, tax, and legal considerations are reviewed** | You have a refund policy, understand payment fees, and have general tax awareness | Refund policy written; payment processor chosen; tax awareness (Section 12) |
| 8 | **There is a clear maintenance plan** | You know how you will fix bugs, update features, and keep the app running | Weekly check commitment; monitoring set up |
| 9 | **There is a safe data handling policy** | You know what happens to user data if they cancel, if you shut down, or if there is a breach | Privacy notice exists; data export possible; deletion plan known |
| 10 | **There is a plan for user onboarding and support** | New users can figure out how to use the app; they know how to get help | Onboarding flow exists; support email/form/chat exists |

### 🍳 Analogy

> **The Commercial Readiness Gate is like a health inspection for your food stall.** You can cook amazing food, but if you do not have clean water, proper storage, and a way to handle complaints — you are not ready to serve the public safely.

## 7. If NOT Ready — What to Do Instead

If you fail the Commercial Readiness Gate, you are NOT stuck. You have options.

| Option | What It Means | Best When |
|--------|-------------|-----------|
| **Continue as personal tool** | Keep using it yourself. Improve it. Show it in your portfolio. | The problem is real for YOU, but unproven for others |
| **Offer as manual service** | Deliver the value yourself (consulting, done-for-you) while building the tool | People want the outcome but the app is not ready |
| **Sell a template/digital product** | Package your knowledge as a Notion template, spreadsheet, or guide | You know the domain well, even if the app is not ready |
| **Run a waitlist** | Collect interest. Build in public. Launch when ready. | You need more validation before building payments |
| **Do a paid pilot** | Charge 3–5 users a discounted price for early access + feedback | The app works but is rough — users get a deal in exchange for feedback |
| **Keep as portfolio-only** | Show it publicly as a portfolio project, do not charge | The project demonstrates skill even if it is not monetizable |

## 8. "Before Charging Money" Checklist

From the Playbook §22 — every item must be true before you accept payment from anyone.

- [ ] ✅ **Know your refund policy** — What happens if someone wants their money back? Write it down.
- [ ] ✅ **Know your support channel** — How do users reach you when something breaks? (Email, form, chat)
- [ ] ✅ **Know what data you collect** — List every piece of data your app collects from users.
- [ ] ✅ **Know your privacy obligations** — Do you need a privacy policy page? (Yes, if you collect any personal data)
- [ ] ✅ **Keep sales records** — Track every payment, refund, and invoice. You will need this for taxes.
- [ ] ✅ **Check local tax/legal requirements** — In the Philippines: BIR registration, income tax, percentage tax, or VAT depending on revenue level.
- [ ] ✅ **Avoid promising guaranteed results** — Never say "guaranteed to save you ₱X" or "guaranteed to work." Use honest language about benefits.

### Additional Items (from Master Prompt §22)

- [ ] ✅ **Test the payment flow in test mode** before going live — Lemon Squeezy and Stripe both have test modes.
- [ ] ✅ **Verify your payment processor works for your country** — Stripe may not work from PH; Lemon Squeezy payout details for PH should be manually confirmed.
- [ ] ✅ **Have a maintenance plan** — Who fixes bugs after launch? How quickly?
- [ ] ✅ **Have a user onboarding plan** — Can a new user figure out the app without your help?

## 9. How to Avoid Building Payments Too Early

### The Timing Guide

| Stage | Should You Build Payments? | What to Do Instead |
|-------|--------------------------|-------------------|
| 💡 **Idea** | ❌ No | Validate the problem first |
| 🔍 **Validation** | ❌ No | Test willingness to pay with surveys and pricing page clicks |
| 🎨 **Prototype** | ❌ No | Show the prototype, ask if people would pay |
| 🔧 **MVP (self-use)** | ❌ No | Use it yourself first, invite testers |
| 🧪 **Private beta (5–20 users)** | ⚠️ Maybe — manual paid pilot only | Manual invoice / bank transfer for pilot users |
| 🚀 **Public MVP** | ✅ Yes — if Commercial Readiness Gate is passed | Add Lemon Squeezy or PayMongo checkout |
| 💰 **Monetized MVP** | ✅ Yes | Formal payment system, refund policy, support channel |

### 🍳 Analogy

> **Adding payments is like hiring a cashier for your food stall.** You do not hire a cashier when you are still testing the recipe. You hire a cashier when people are lining up and asking "how much?"

### Why Early Payments Are Dangerous

| Risk | What Happens |
|------|-------------|
| **Refund headaches** | Broken features → refund requests → stress |\
| **Tax surprise** | Earning money creates tax obligations you may not be ready for |
| **Support burden** | Paying users expect support — are you ready for that? |
| **Legal exposure** | Accepting money without proper terms, refund policy, or data handling creates legal risk |
| **Distraction** | Time spent on payment bugs = time NOT spent on making the product better |

## 10. How to Separate Free vs. Paid Features

### The Freemium Framework

If you decide on a freemium model (free plan + paid plan), use this framework:

| Layer | Free Plan (Hook) | Paid Plan (Value) |
|-------|-----------------|-------------------|
| **Core action** | ✅ Available — limited quantity | ✅ Available — unlimited |
| **Data storage** | Limited (e.g., 10 records) | More (e.g., 1,000 records) |
| **Features** | Basic features only | Advanced features (export, analytics, integrations) |
| **Support** | Community / self-serve | Email / priority support |
| **Branding** | "Built with [Your App]" badge | No badge |
| **Users / seats** | 1 user | Multiple users / team |

### The Decision Rule

> **Give away enough for free that users experience the value. Charge for the things that make them use it MORE or BETTER.**

### Example: Tutor Notes App

| Feature | Free | Pro (₱299/mo) |
|---------|------|--------------|
| Students | Up to 5 | Unlimited |
| Session notes | Last 30 days | All history |
| Search | ✅ | ✅ |
| CSV export | ❌ | ✅ |
| Reminders | ❌ | ✅ |
| Support | Help page | Email support |

## 11. Pricing Research Basics

### How to Figure Out What to Charge

| Step | Action | Tool |
|------|--------|------|
| 1 | **Find 3–5 competitors/alternatives** | Google search, Product Hunt, Reddit |
| 2 | **List their prices** | Check their pricing pages — take screenshots for your Source Ledger |
| 3 | **Identify the price range** | What is the cheapest? Most expensive? Average? |
| 4 | **Position yourself** | As a new/unknown product, price at or below the average |
| 5 | **Start simple** | One price, one plan. Add tiers later if needed. |
| 6 | **Test and adjust** | After 50 users or 3 months, consider adjusting |

### Pricing Mental Models

| Model | When to Use | Example |
|-------|------------|---------|
| **Cost + margin** | When you have clear costs (hosting, API fees) | Costs ₱500/mo → charge ₱1,500/mo (3x) |
| **Value-based** | When you save users time or money | Saves 10 hrs/month × ₱200/hr = ₱2,000 value → charge ₱500 (25%) |
| **Competitor match** | When similar tools exist | Competitors charge $10–$30/mo → price at $15/mo |
| **"Would you pay ₱X?"** | When you have no data | Ask 10 users: "Would you pay ₱99, ₱299, or ₱599?" — see what sticks |

### Beginner Pricing Tips

| Tip | Why |
|-----|-----|
| **Start low → raise later** | Easier to raise prices than lower them (early users get a deal) |
| **Use round numbers** | ₱299/mo is easier to decide on than ₱287.50/mo |
| **Offer annual = 2 months free** | ₱299/mo or ₱2,990/year — rewards commitment |
| **Do not charge ₱0.99** | It signals "this is not worth much." Charge what it is worth. |
| **Include tax in the displayed price** | ₱299 total, not ₱265 + tax. Simpler for buyers. |

## 12. Tax and Legal General Caution

> **⚠️ This section provides GENERAL AWARENESS only. It is NOT tax or legal advice. Consult a qualified PH accountant or lawyer before making business decisions.**

### What You Should Be Aware Of

| Topic | General Guidance |
|-------|-----------------|
| **Income tax** | Earning money = taxable income in the Philippines. This applies even for online/digital income. |
| **BIR registration** | If you earn regularly, you may need to register with the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR). |
| **Percentage tax** | Small businesses below the VAT threshold may pay 3% percentage tax on gross receipts (verify current rate). |
| **VAT** | If gross receipts exceed the VAT threshold (currently ₱3,000,000/year — verify), you must register for VAT (12%). |
| **Official receipts** | You may need to issue official receipts for payments received. |
| **Business permits** | Depending on your city (Manila, Makati, etc.), you may need a barangay clearance, mayor's permit, etc. |
| **Data privacy** | The Data Privacy Act of 2012 (RA 10173) applies if you collect personal data from users in the Philippines. |
| **Refund obligations** | You should have a written refund policy. The Consumer Act of the Philippines may apply. |
| **Terms of service** | You should have a terms of service page that explains what users agree to when using your app. |
| **International income** | If you earn in USD (e.g., Lemon Squeezy payouts), this is still taxable in PH. Exchange rate at time of receipt matters. |

### When to Get Professional Help

| Milestone | Action |
|-----------|--------|
| Before first paid pilot | Read general BIR guides for freelancers/solopreneurs |
| Before accepting regular payments | Consult a PH accountant about BIR registration and tax obligations |
| Before reaching ₱500K+ annual revenue | Definitely have an accountant on retainer |
| Before any legal issue (refund dispute, data breach, etc.) | Consult a lawyer |

## 13. Monetization Readiness Checklist — Complete

This combined checklist merges ALL requirements from the Master Prompt §22 and Playbook §9 and §22.

### 🔍 Validation

- [ ] At least 5–10 target users validated the problem
- [ ] At least 3 users showed serious willingness to pay (not just "sounds cool")
- [ ] The tool solves a repeated painful problem (not a one-time inconvenience)
- [ ] You can explain the value in one clear sentence (Product North Star)

### 💰 Payment

- [ ] Payment processor chosen (Lemon Squeezy, PayMongo, Stripe, or manual)
- [ ] Payment processor works for your country (PH: check Stripe, verify Lemon Squeezy payouts)
- [ ] Payment flow tested in test mode
- [ ] Refund policy written and accessible
- [ ] Pricing is clear and displayed on your app/site
- [ ] Receipts/invoices are generated (automatic or manual)

### 📋 Legal / Tax / Privacy

- [ ] Know your local tax obligations (general awareness — consult accountant before scaling)
- [ ] Know if you need BIR registration for regular income
- [ ] Privacy policy page exists (if collecting personal data)
- [ ] Terms of service page exists
- [ ] Know what data you collect and why
- [ ] Know your privacy obligations under Philippine Data Privacy Act
- [ ] Avoid promising guaranteed results in marketing
- [ ] Keep sales records (every payment, refund, invoice tracked)

### 🔧 Product

- [ ] Main flow works reliably (tested by 5+ users)
- [ ] Support channel exists (email, form, or chat)
- [ ] Support burden is manageable for a solo operator
- [ ] Onboarding is clear — new users can figure out the app
- [ ] Bug fix and maintenance plan exists (weekly check commitment)
- [ ] Security and privacy risks are understood and addressed
- [ ] Data handling policy exists (what happens to user data on cancellation/shutdown)
- [ ] Monitoring is set up (Sentry, UptimeRobot, or equivalent)

### 🚀 Launch

- [ ] Commercial Readiness Gate — all 10 criteria passed
- [ ] "Before Charging Money" checklist — all 7 items confirmed
- [ ] Hosting platform allows commercial use (NOT Vercel Hobby)
- [ ] Analytics tracking key events (signup, core action, payment)
- [ ] Feedback collection method exists (Tally form or in-app)
